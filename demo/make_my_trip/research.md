# Research: Make My Trip

## Case Archetype
improve_product

## Company Context
MakeMyTrip Limited (NASDAQ: MMYT) is India's leading online travel company, serving travelers since 2000, and operates a multi-brand portfolio: MakeMyTrip (flights/hotels/packages), Goibibo, redBus (bus/train), and myBiz (corporate travel). It is the clear #1 online travel agency (OTA) in India by gross bookings, with competitors (Ixigo, Cleartrip, EaseMyTrip, Yatra) each holding an estimated 7-9% market share versus MakeMyTrip's dominant position (commonly cited as 50%+ of the OTA market).

Financially, MakeMyTrip delivered record results in FY25 (year ended March 31, 2025): $9.8 billion in gross bookings (+25.9% YoY constant currency), $978.3 million in IFRS revenue (+27.4% YoY), and $167.3 million in adjusted operating profit (EBIT), up from $124.2 million in FY24. However, momentum cooled in Q1 FY26 (quarter ended June 2026): revenue grew only 6.2% YoY reported (16.1% in constant currency) as a >10% INR depreciation and subdued international outbound demand (linked to the West Asia conflict) weighed on results, and profit fell to $9.1 million from $25.8 million due to higher convertible-note finance costs.

Strategically, MakeMyTrip has gone "AI-first" over the past 18 months. It launched Myra, a generative AI travel assistant that lets users plan and book trips via voice, text, image, or video, and upgraded it to "Myra 2.0" — an agentic AI that can complete an entire booking (search, refine, pay) inside one conversational thread, with full voice support in eight Indian languages. In February 2026, MakeMyTrip announced a collaboration with OpenAI, integrating OpenAI's APIs into Myra to capture "AI-led discovery" traffic (i.e., when travelers start planning via ChatGPT-like conversational tools) and convert it directly into bookable transactions — a defensive/offensive move as LLM-based assistants threaten to disintermediate traditional OTA search funnels. Co-founder and Group CEO Rajesh Magow has stated AI is central to the company's strategy to enhance customer experience and improve productivity, and that Myra is designed to fuse live inventory data with "human context" to personalize at the point of intent.

Other recent strategic moves: continued Tier-2/Tier-3 city penetration (a primary growth vector, with Myra usage skewing 45%+ from smaller cities), international outbound expansion (now ~25% of revenue, growing much faster than domestic — international air ticketing revenue +33% YoY and international hotels +65% YoY in FY25), and growth of ancillary/adjacent revenue lines (forex, travel insurance, intercity cabs, train bookings — up ~30% YoY in constant currency) and the myBiz corporate travel vertical.

## Business Model / Flywheel
MakeMyTrip operates a classic multi-sided OTA marketplace/commission-and-margin model: it earns money primarily through (1) adjusted margin on air ticketing (commissions + fare markup/incentives from airlines), (2) adjusted margin on hotels & packages (commission/markup on room nights and holiday packages, plus growing hotel-direct/dynamic packaging economics), (3) bus and train ticketing commissions (via redBus and its rail platform), and (4) "other" ancillary revenue (forex, insurance, cabs, visa services, advertising from hotels/airlines for placement). Revenue is a function of Gross Bookings × take rate, and the FY25 numbers show revenue (~$978M) growing roughly in line with gross bookings (~$9.8B), implying a blended take rate near 10%.

The core flywheel: More travelers transact on the platform → more booking/behavioral data (search patterns, price sensitivity, itinerary preferences, repeat travel patterns) → better personalization, pricing, and recommendation (increasingly AI-driven via Myra) → higher conversion and higher average order value (cross-sell of hotels+flights+cabs+forex+insurance) → higher margin dollars per user, which MakeMyTrip reinvests into marketing/customer acquisition and supply-side negotiation (better hotel rates, more airline inventory, exclusive packages) → this supply/price advantage + brand trust attracts more travelers, especially first-time/Tier-2/3 users → cycle repeats. Scale also gives MakeMyTrip negotiating leverage with airlines/hotels (volume commitments), which smaller competitors like EaseMyTrip and Yatra cannot match, reinforcing its dominant share.

A secondary, AI-specific flywheel is now emerging: More conversational interactions with Myra → richer intent-level data (not just clicks but *why* a user wants to travel, budget constraints, accessibility needs, visa status) → sharper agentic recommendations and higher-converting bookings (MakeMyTrip already reports ~10% higher conversion via Myra vs. standard flow) → more users adopt Myra as default entry point (voice-first appeals strongly to Tier-2/3 and first-time digital travelers, who over-index 45%+ of Myra usage) → MakeMyTrip captures demand before it leaks to generic LLM assistants (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews) that could otherwise disintermediate the OTA layer entirely — hence the OpenAI partnership, designed to make MakeMyTrip the "transaction layer" behind AI-native discovery rather than be bypassed by it.

A key vulnerability in the flywheel: heavy reliance on marketing/promotional spend to keep the flywheel spinning — sales & marketing expense grew 34% YoY in FY25 (to $165.3M) — meaning growth is not purely organic/network-effect driven yet; discounting and customer inducement remain a meaningful lever, and AI is explicitly pitched by leadership as a way to improve "productivity" (i.e., lower the cost of acquiring and serving each incremental booking) alongside enhancing experience.

## Market Analysis
Market sizing estimates for India's online travel market vary significantly by research firm, which is itself a useful case-interview data point (a candidate should sanity-check any single source, and note the wide range in the "TAM" is real ambiguity, not error):
- IMARC Group: USD 55.74 billion (2025) → USD 60.87 billion (2026) → USD 126.01 billion by 2034 (9.2% CAGR); explicitly attributes the 2025-2026 jump partly to "AI-native booking assistants" and Tier-2/3 adoption.
- Mordor Intelligence: USD 23.34 billion (2025) → USD 25.38 billion (2026) → USD 38.58 billion by 2031 (8.74% CAGR).
- Research and Markets: USD 19.05 billion (2025) → USD 31.38 billion by 2030 (10.5% CAGR).
- GII Research: USD 23.1 billion (2025) → USD 33.9 billion by 2030 (7.76% CAGR).
- Verified Market Research: >$51 billion (2024) → $124 billion by 2033.
Given MakeMyTrip's own FY25 gross bookings of $9.8B, the more conservative $19-25B "online travel market" estimates (excluding some adjacent categories) appear more internally consistent with an implied ~40-50% share of the addressable online OTA segment — consistent with commentary that MakeMyTrip holds "over 50% market share" of India's OTA space, while competitors (Ixigo, Cleartrip, EaseMyTrip, Yatra) cluster in the high-single-digit percentages each.

Growth drivers cited across sources: rising disposable incomes, mobile-first booking behavior, UPI-enabled payments, expanding Tier-2/Tier-3 smartphone adoption (India has 900M+ wireless data users), a growing "bleisure" (business + leisure) segment, and — increasingly — AI-native booking assistants as a distinct growth catalyst rather than just a UX nicety. Domestic air passenger traffic in India is projected to double by 2030 per government infrastructure initiatives, underpinning continued volume growth even before wallet-share gains from AI-driven cross-sell.

SAM for an "AI-improvement" lens: the relevant addressable opportunity for a PM proposing AI features is not the full TAM but the slice of gross bookings/revenue that AI can influence — (a) reducing funnel drop-off/cart abandonment (industry-wide travel booking abandonment is a well-documented problem, often cited around 80%+ across travel e-commerce broadly, though MakeMyTrip-specific figures are not public), (b) reducing customer support cost-to-serve (support/refund complaints are the #1 driver of MakeMyTrip's poor Trustpilot/PissedConsumer ratings), and (c) capturing incremental demand from Tier-2/3 and first-time digital travelers who most benefit from voice/vernacular AI — a segment Myra is already over-indexing on (45%+ of usage from smaller cities).

SOM: MakeMyTrip's own data suggests AI (Myra) is already driving a measurable SOM today — 3M+ conversations/quarter, ~10% higher conversion for Myra-assisted bookings, and 35%+ of travelers engaging Myra up to 90 days pre-trip — implying AI-influenced bookings are already a material and growing slice of the $9.8B gross bookings base, likely to be a primary growth lever going into FY26/FY27 as agentic Myra 2.0 and the OpenAI integration scale.

## Competitor Landscape
India's OTA market is a differentiated oligopoly with MakeMyTrip as the dominant leader and a cluster of mid-sized challengers, plus indirect competition from vertical specialists and global players:

1. MakeMyTrip (+ Goibibo, redBus) — Market leader, full-stack OTA (flights, hotels, packages, bus, train, forex, insurance, cabs, corporate travel via myBiz). AI moat: Myra/Myra 2.0 agentic assistant (3M+ conversations/quarter, 8-language voice support, OpenAI-powered discovery capture). Heavy investment in Tier-2/3 penetration. Weakness: customer service reputation is poor (1.2-1.4 star ratings on Trustpilot/PissedConsumer), with recurring complaints about phantom bookings, forced wallet refunds, and AI-only support that frustrates users with real emergencies.

2. Ixigo (Le Travenues Technology) — Fastest-growing challenger and emergent #2 by some measures, strong in trains/buses (via AbhiBus, ConfirmTkt) plus flights/hotels. Rebuilt its entire app as "AI-native" in May 2026 with a multimodal assistant TARA that plans, books, and acts agentically — including proactive post-booking assistance (flight tracking, automatic cancellations/refunds, real-time alerts) via a "Trip Mode." TARA resolved 3.81M customer queries in Q3 FY26 (up from 2.11M in Q4 FY25), with 76% of voice calls fully AI-resolved — arguably ahead of MakeMyTrip on AI-driven post-booking service, a key differentiation angle since Ixigo positions AI as reducing human support load, not just aiding discovery. Trades at a premium valuation (P/E ~119x) reflecting growth expectations, versus EaseMyTrip (~36x) and Yatra (~40x).

3. Cleartrip (Flipkart-backed) — Repositioned as a value/simplicity player, competing for the #2/#3 spot; benefits from Flipkart's distribution and payments ecosystem integration.

4. EaseMyTrip — Historically air-ticketing-heavy (~90%+ revenue from flights), asset-light/no-discounting positioning, growing hotels business fast (+189% YoY in Q4 FY25) off a small base; less differentiated on AI relative to MakeMyTrip/Ixigo.

5. Yatra — NASDAQ-listed, more corporate/B2B travel-weighted, moderate growth (Q2FY25 revenue-less-service-cost of Rs 94 crore, +20% QoQ).

6. IRCTC — Not a direct OTA competitor but the dominant channel for rail e-ticketing (~80% direct market share), meaning MakeMyTrip/redBus/Ixigo compete only for the ~20% of rail bookings that flow through third-party OTAs — a structural ceiling worth noting for any "expand into trains" AI angle.

7. Global players (Booking.com, Agoda, Expedia) — Compete mainly on inbound/international hotel supply and international outbound bookings, an area where MakeMyTrip is investing heavily (int'l hotels revenue +65% YoY FY25) partly to defend against these players' superior global hotel inventory and review depth.

Key competitive AI positioning takeaway: Ixigo's TARA is explicitly framed as "agent-led" and proactive/post-booking (managing disruptions, refunds, real-time alerts), while MakeMyTrip's Myra has so far emphasized pre-booking discovery-to-purchase conversion (voice-first planning, OpenAI-powered discovery capture). This suggests MakeMyTrip's AI investment has a gap on the post-booking/in-trip/customer-service side — exactly where its Trustpilot/PissedConsumer complaints concentrate (refunds, disruptions, unresponsive support) — while Ixigo is directly attacking that pain point with AI.

## User Segments
1. First-time/Tier-2 & Tier-3 Digital Traveler ("The Emerging Explorer") — JTBD: "Help me book a trip confidently even though I'm not fluent in English, unfamiliar with complex fare/filter UX, and anxious about getting scammed or making an irreversible mistake." This segment already over-indexes in Myra usage (45%+ of Myra conversations, ~50% higher voice usage than metros) because voice/vernacular interaction lowers the literacy and confidence barrier of a filter-heavy interface. Pain points: distrust of online payments, difficulty parsing fare rules/cancellation terms, and needing reassurance in their own language before committing to a purchase (this is likely why Myra 2.0 rolled out 8-language voice support and passport-image autofill — reducing form-filling friction that disproportionately hurts this segment).

2. Metro Value/Frequent Leisure Traveler ("The Deal Optimizer") — JTBD: "Help me quickly find the best combination of price, timing, and loyalty benefit across flights + hotels without me manually comparing multiple apps/tabs." This segment is more price- and convenience-sensitive, drives repeat bookings (part of the 75M lifetime transacted user base), and is the target of loyalty mechanics like MMT Black. Pain points: fatigue from "filter and scroll" search UX, price changes mid-booking-flow (a recurring Trustpilot complaint — "price changed twice after I selected the flight"), and lack of proactive rebooking/refund help when plans change.

3. International Outbound & Business/Bleisure Traveler ("The Complex Itinerary Planner") — JTBD: "Help me plan and execute a multi-leg, higher-stakes trip (visas, multiple cities, forex, corporate approvals) without juggling a dozen separate tools." This segment is a strategic growth priority — int'l revenue is ~25% of total and growing 33-65% YoY across categories — and includes myBiz corporate travelers. Pain points: visa requirement uncertainty, need for human-level judgment on complex multi-city/multi-currency itineraries, and (per complaint data) especially poor customer support access "from USA/UK" with "unreachable phone lines" when something goes wrong on an international trip — a segment where the cost of a failure (missed international connection, denied hotel check-in abroad) is much higher than domestic bookings.

Common cross-segment pain point (JTBD failure mode): across Trustpilot, PissedConsumer, and App Store reviews, the single most repeated complaint is not discovery/search — it's what happens when something goes wrong (hotel doesn't honor booking, flight cancelled, cab no-show) and the primary interface offered is an AI/automated chatbot that customers feel is "so frustrating," unable to escalate to a human, and offers only wallet credit instead of cash refunds. This suggests the JTBD MakeMyTrip is currently under-serving is not "help me find a trip" (well solved by Myra) but "help me recover trust and get made whole when the trip breaks."

## User Journey
Primary segment: Tier-2/3 first-time or infrequent digital traveler using the MakeMyTrip app for a domestic leisure trip (hotel + flight/bus).

1. Discovery/Entry: User opens the app (often after seeing a WhatsApp/social ad or hearing about a destination). With Myra 2.0, users can now start in a conversational box (text or voice, in 8 languages) rather than a search form. Friction point (pre-Myra 2.0 / for non-adopters): traditional filter-and-scroll UX requires literacy in fare terminology and English-heavy filters, discouraging first-time users — this is precisely why Myra's usage skews toward Tier-2/3 (45%+) and why voice usage there is ~50% higher than metros.

2. Intent capture & search: User describes trip in natural language ("family trip to Goa in December, budget hotel near beach"). Myra parses intent and surfaces structured, transaction-ready options combining flights + hotels. Moment of delight: multimodal input (e.g., uploading a passport image for auto-fill) removes tedious manual form entry, a known friction point for less digitally fluent users. Friction point (legacy flow): dynamic pricing can shift mid-session — a frequently cited complaint is prices changing after a flight is selected and passenger details are being entered, which erodes trust right at the point of commitment.

3. Refinement/mid-journey questions: User pauses to ask contextual questions (e.g., accessibility, visa requirements, "does this hotel have parking?") without losing their place — a capability explicitly built into Myra 2.0 to address the old problem of resetting search filters every time a question arose. Unmet need still present: judging by review data, users still don't trust "confirmed" status enough (multiple complaints about hotels having "no record of our booking" despite app confirmation and card charge) — indicating a real-time inventory/confirmation-integrity gap between MakeMyTrip's system and supplier systems, not just a UX problem.

4. Booking & payment: With Myra 2.0, booking, seat/room selection, traveler info, and payment can now happen within a single conversational/tap-based flow, reducing tab-switching. This is a genuine improvement — MakeMyTrip reports ~10% higher conversion for Myra-assisted bookings than the standard flow.

5. Confirmation & pre-trip: 35%+ of users re-engage Myra up to 90 days before departure (itinerary questions, visa checks), and 1-in-4 return specifically to ask follow-up questions — showing the AI assistant has become a genuine pre-trip utility, not just a booking tool. This is a high-engagement, high-trust moment MakeMyTrip could extend further (e.g., proactive nudges) but currently seems to under-leverage for post-booking risk reduction (e.g., proactively confirming with the hotel/supplier).

6. In-trip/disruption handling (major drop-off/frustration point): This is where the journey breaks down most severely based on review evidence. When a flight is cancelled, a cab doesn't show up, or a hotel refuses a "confirmed" booking, users are routed into an "AI-based" chatbot support flow that reviewers describe as frustrating and unable to resolve real emergencies (e.g., a same-day medical emergency, a 3:35am cab cancellation before a flight). There is no clear, fast human escalation path, and disputed refunds are often issued as non-cash wallet credit rather than original payment method — a major trust violation flagged repeatedly across Trustpilot/PissedConsumer/ComplaintsBoard.

7. Post-trip: Limited evidence of proactive post-trip engagement (e.g., automatic re-booking suggestions, loyalty recognition) beyond MMT Black loyalty tiers; the qualitative data suggests most post-trip "engagement" that exists today is actually unresolved complaint follow-up, not delight-driven re-engagement.

Overall friction map: MakeMyTrip's AI investment (Myra) has meaningfully improved steps 1-5 (discovery through booking) — the "search and buy" journey — but steps 6-7 (disruption handling, refunds, in-trip support) remain the weakest and most reputationally damaging part of the journey, and are notably the exact area where competitor Ixigo's TARA assistant is now investing (proactive disruption management, automated refund processing, real-time alerts).

## Key Insights
1. MakeMyTrip's AI investment has a "front-loaded" bias — it has poured effort into pre-purchase discovery/conversion (Myra's voice-first planning, OpenAI-powered discovery capture) precisely because that's where growth and conversion-rate metrics are easiest to show to investors, but the qualitative complaint data (Trustpilot 1.2/5, PissedConsumer 1.4/5) reveals the actual reputation-destroying moments happen post-booking (phantom bookings, cancellations, forced wallet refunds). A differentiated AI solution should target the "AI as claims adjuster / trip guardian" opportunity — proactively re-confirming bookings with suppliers, predicting disruption risk, and auto-initiating real refunds — rather than adding more discovery polish where MakeMyTrip already leads.

2. Competitor Ixigo's TARA is already moving into this exact whitespace (proactive disruption management, 76% AI-resolved voice calls, automated refund processing) — meaning MakeMyTrip's post-booking AI gap is not hypothetical, it's a live competitive threat from the #2 player, not just an internal opportunity. This reframes "improve MakeMyTrip with AI" from a generic UX exercise into a defensive imperative against a fast-moving challenger.

3. The OpenAI collaboration signals a strategic fear, not just an opportunity: MakeMyTrip explicitly framed the deal around capturing "high-intent travel queries" and moving from "passive search visibility to active participation in AI-led discovery" — i.e., they are worried that ChatGPT/Gemini-style assistants will intercept travel intent before it ever reaches the MakeMyTrip app, disintermediating them the way Google/aggregators disintermediated hotels. Any AI improvement proposal should explicitly address how MakeMyTrip stays the "transaction layer of record" as consumer AI habits shift toward asking a general assistant first.

4. There's a monetizable trust asymmetry in the Tier-2/3 segment: this segment is the fastest-growing and most AI-engaged (45%+ of Myra usage, 50% higher voice usage) but is also the segment least equipped to detect/dispute a "phantom booking" scam or fight for a cash refund over a wallet credit — meaning AI-driven trust/verification features (e.g., real-time supplier confirmation, proactive fraud/mismatch detection before charging the card) would disproportionately serve MakeMyTrip's core growth segment and could be marketed as a genuine differentiator versus incumbents who treat Tier-2/3 mainly as a volume/discount play.

5. The wide dispersion in "market size" estimates ($19B–$56B depending on source) combined with MakeMyTrip's own disclosed $9.8B gross bookings suggests MakeMyTrip already commands an unusually high (40-50%+) share of the *addressable* online segment — meaning further growth is less about winning new OTA market share (a shrinking prize) and more about (a) expanding the total pie via first-time/Tier-2-3 digitization (where AI/voice is the actual unlock) and (b) increasing wallet share per existing traveler via cross-sell (forex, insurance, cabs — already the fastest-growing "other" category, +30% YoY) — both of which point toward an AI-driven personalization/bundling agent rather than pure marketing-spend-driven acquisition (notable given marketing spend already grew 34% YoY, outpacing revenue growth in some periods).
