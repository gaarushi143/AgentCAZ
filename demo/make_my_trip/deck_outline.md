# Deck Outline: MakeMyTrip — "How would you improve MakeMyTrip with AI?"

**Narrative arc:** Leader with nowhere left to grow by share → its AI is front-loaded onto the problem it already solved → the post-booking journey is unmanaged and reputationally toxic → the #2 player is already attacking that exact gap → reframe: reallocate AI from selling to guaranteeing → three options, RICE → build Myra Guardian → why only MMT can build it → metrics, rollout, risks.

---

- **Headline**: From Salesperson to Fiduciary: Using AI to Fix the Half of the Trip MakeMyTrip Doesn't Manage
- **Purpose**: Frame the entire deck with a point of view before a single data slide, so the interviewer knows this is a judgment answer, not a feature list.
- **Convey**: The interesting AI opportunity at MakeMyTrip is post-booking, not discovery.
- **Content**: Company logo + case question verbatim ("How would you improve MakeMyTrip with AI?"); thesis line in one sentence: "Myra sells the trip brilliantly. Nothing owns it after payment. That's where the 1.2/5 rating — and the growth — lives."; presenter name, date, role applied for.
- **Visuals**: Full-bleed clean title layout; small horizontal journey ribbon along the bottom with steps 1–7, steps 1–5 in MMT red/solid and steps 6–7 in grey/dashed — a visual teaser of the argument.

## Slide 3: Problem
- **Headline**: Myra Fixed Steps 1–5 of the Journey. Steps 6–7 Have No AI at All — and a 1.2/5 Rating to Show It
- **Purpose**: The core problem slide. Show that MMT's AI investment and MMT's customer pain sit on opposite ends of the same journey.
- **Convey**: Users aren't churning because search is hard; they're churning because a card-charged "confirmed" booking turned out not to exist and a chatbot offered wallet credit.
- **Content**: Top half — 7-step journey heatmap with status per step: Discovery/Search/Refine/Book/Pre-trip = SOLVED (evidence: 3M+ Myra conversations/quarter, 8-language voice, ~10% higher conversion, 45%+ usage from Tier-2/3, 35% engage 90 days pre-trip); In-trip disruption/Post-trip = UNMANAGED. Bottom half — evidence panel: Trustpilot 1.2/5 (2,000+ reviews), PissedConsumer 1.4/5 (1,016 reviews, only 17% would recommend); three verbatim complaint themes as quote cards: "hotel had no record of our booking" (phantom bookings), "refund only as wallet credit, not to my card," "AI chatbot with no way to reach a human" (3:35am cab no-show, same-day medical emergency).
- **Visuals**: Horizontal 7-stage journey funnel with red/green heat shading on each stage, AI-coverage icons on stages 1–5 and empty slots on 6–7; three complaint quote cards beneath with source ratings as gauge badges.

## Slide 5: Key Insight
- **Headline**: MakeMyTrip's AI Spend and MakeMyTrip's Customer Pain Are in Different Places
- **Purpose**: The pivot slide — the single non-obvious reframe that makes the rest of the deck inevitable. Also states the scoped problem being solved.
- **Convey**: The obvious answer is more Myra discovery; the right answer is to reallocate AI from selling the trip to guaranteeing it.
- **Content**: Top: two-sided insight statement — "Where AI is deployed: pre-purchase discovery (easiest metric to show investors)" vs "Where trust is destroyed: post-booking (3,000+ public reviews)." Middle: the monetizable trust asymmetry — the Tier-2/3 segment is the most AI-engaged (45%+ of Myra usage) and the least able to detect a phantom booking or contest a wallet credit. Bottom: the scoped problem statement in a boxed callout — "Reduce post-booking trust failure for ~35M annual transacting users, starting with the Tier-2/3 first-time traveler, by using AI to (1) verify supplier-side booking integrity before arrival, (2) predict and pre-empt disruption, (3) adjudicate and refund to source automatically — so AI cuts cost-to-serve and raises repeat rate at the same time."
- **Visuals**: Simple two-arrow diagram: an "AI investment" arrow pointing at the front of the journey and a "customer pain" arrow pointing at the back, with the mismatch gap highlighted; scoped problem in a bordered box below. Minimal, insight-forward — deliberately the lightest data slide in the deck.

## Slide 7: Solution Options
- **Headline**: Three Candidate AI Bets: Guarantee the Trip, De-Risk the Purchase, or Defend the Funnel
- **Purpose**: Demonstrate breadth before depth — prove the recommendation was chosen against real alternatives, not pulled from thin air.
- **Convey**: All three are credible AI plays, and they attack three structurally different problems: post-booking trust, pre-commit anxiety, and strategic disintermediation.
- **Content**: Three feature cards, each with name / one-line description / pain point addressed / flywheel effect. (1) **Myra Guardian** — always-on post-booking agent: verify → predict → make whole; attacks the #1 complaint theme across Trustpilot/PissedConsumer. (2) **Bharosa Mode** — vernacular trust layer at the pre-payment moment: spoken fare rules, 10-minute Price Lock, pre-pay sanity check (name/date/airport mismatch); attacks fare-rule illiteracy and "price changed twice after I selected the flight." (3) **Intent Bridge** — turn the OpenAI collaboration into a distribution channel: agentic commerce endpoint, WhatsApp-native Myra, 90-day-out cross-sell into forex/insurance/cabs (+30% YoY CC); attacks a *company* risk, not a user pain — general assistants intercepting intent.
- **Visuals**: Three vertical feature cards side by side, each tagged with the journey stage it touches (post-booking / commit / discovery) using the same journey-ribbon color coding from Slides 1, 3 and 4 for continuity.

## Slide 8: Prioritization
- **Headline**: Guardian Wins on RICE (2.92M) — and the Tie-Breaker Isn't RICE, It's Moat
- **Purpose**: Show quantitative rigor *and* the intellectual honesty to admit RICE doesn't decide a 20% gap — then decide it with judgment.
- **Convey**: Guardian ranks first on the numbers, and the close call against Bharosa Mode is settled by defensibility and sequencing logic.
- **Content**: RICE table with stated assumptions header (Reach = unique users materially touched/year; Impact 0.25–3; Effort in engineer-months; baseline ~35M annual transacting users, ~65M annual transactions). Rows — Guardian: 35M × 2.5 × 0.8 / 24 = **2.92M**; Bharosa Mode: 15M × 1.5 × 0.85 / 8 = **2.39M**; Intent Bridge: 6M × 1.0 × 0.5 / 12 = **0.25M**. Right-hand "Honest read" panel: the top two are within ~20% — inside estimation noise. Two tie-breakers: **Defensibility** (Bharosa Mode is copyable in a quarter by any 7–9% player; Guardian needs supplier integration depth + refund rails only a 50%-share player can assemble — RICE measures value, not moat) and **Sequencing** (Bharosa makes people buy; Guardian makes them come back — conversion polish on a 1.2/5 trust base is water into a leaking bucket). Decision line: build Guardian now, Bharosa as an 8-eng-month fast-follow in Phase 2, Intent Bridge as a lean partnership track, not a product investment.
- **Visuals**: RICE table with a horizontal bar of final scores alongside each row (so the 2.92 vs 2.39 closeness is visible, not hidden); Guardian row highlighted; separate callout box for the two tie-breakers.

## Slide 9: Feature Deep-Dive
- **Headline**: Myra Guardian: Verify Before Arrival, Predict Before Failure, Refund Before They Ask
- **Purpose**: The payoff slide. Show the recommended solution is concretely engineered, not a slogan.
- **Convey**: Guardian is three specific, testable loops plus one non-negotiable safety valve — and it replaces today's hollow "confirmed" with a verified state.
- **Content**: Four stacked mechanism blocks. (1) **Booking Assurance loop** — supplier handshake at T+60min and T-24h via PMS/extranet API, plus agentic outbound voice/WhatsApp call to the front desk in the local language for the no-API Tier-2/3 long tail; if no record, auto-rebook into equal-or-better *before* travel; output is a live "Booking Assured ✓" status replacing today's "confirmed." (2) **Disruption Prediction loop** — scores each leg using flight status, schedule-change feeds, weather, cab driver-accept latency, and Supplier Reliability Score; above threshold Myra acts first (pre-emptive cab reassignment, pre-loaded alternate flights, hotel late-arrival notice) with one-tap choices in the user's language. (3) **Make-Whole loop** — AI claims adjudicator classifies the failure, applies fare rules, auto-refunds **to the original payment method within 24 hours** below a value threshold; cash by default, wallet only by choice. (4) **"Human in 60 Seconds"** — intent + sentiment classifier detects stranded / medical / same-day / abroad contexts, skips the bot entirely, routes to a live agent with full trip context pre-loaded. Ships Phase 1, not Phase 3.
- **Visuals**: Horizontal timeline diagram of a single trip (Booking → T+60min → T-24h → In-trip → Resolution) with each of the three loops drawn as an intervention arc firing at its trigger point; "Human in 60 Seconds" drawn as a red escape hatch available at every stage. Before/after UI chip comparison in the corner: grey "Confirmed" vs green "Booking Assured ✓".

## Slide 10: Competitive Advantage
- **Headline**: Ixigo Can Build the Assistant. Only MakeMyTrip Can Build the Handshake
- **Purpose**: Answer the killer interview question — "why can't the competitor just copy this?" — and connect the solution back to the flywheel and the P&L.
- **Convey**: Guardian's hard part is supplier leverage, not the model — which is exactly what 50% share buys and what a 7–9% player cannot replicate. And it pays twice.
- **Content**: Three panels. (1) **The moat**: the difficulty is integration across thousands of hotels/buses/cab partners plus the commercial power to impose reliability scoring and SLA penalties — purchased with $9.8B in gross bookings, not with engineering. "Ixigo's AI can tell you your flight is delayed; only MMT can make thousands of hotels contractually accountable for honoring a verified booking." (2) **It pays twice**: revenue side — +3pp repeat rate on ~65M transactions ≈ 2M incremental transactions ≈ **~$25–30M incremental annual revenue at ~$15 revenue/transaction, with zero incremental CAC**; cost side — 60–70% deflection of post-booking contacts (vs Ixigo's demonstrated 76% voice resolution) cuts cost-to-serve, delivering the "productivity" mandate leadership publicly assigned to AI. (3) **Flywheel fit**: converts bought spin into earned spin at three points — repeat transactions without CAC, a new proprietary data asset (supplier reliability + disruption telemetry no 7–9% player can accumulate) that improves ranking and supply negotiation, and lower support cost releasing margin back into the wheel. Closing note: Guardian is also what makes Intent Bridge worth winning — when a general assistant surfaces every OTA at price parity, the differentiator becomes guarantee quality.
- **Visuals**: Circular flywheel diagram (transactions → data → personalization → conversion/AOV → margin → reinvestment) with three green injection arrows labelled "repeat without CAC," "reliability telemetry," "lower cost-to-serve," and the current weak link ("bought spin: S&M +34.1%") marked in red on the reinvestment arc.

## Slide 12: Rollout Plan
- **Headline**: Prove the Handshake on 10% of Traffic Before Promising a Guarantee to 35M Users
- **Purpose**: Show sequencing discipline — the riskiest assumption is tested first, with a real kill gate, not a gradual feature ramp.
- **Convey**: Coverage is the binding risk, so Phase 1 is deliberately narrow and gated; scale only follows verified proof.
- **Content**: Three phase columns, each with Scope / Audience / Gate. **Phase 1 (M0–3) — Prove the handshake**: supplier re-confirmation via existing API/extranet only; auto-refund-to-source capped ≤₹15,000 for one clean failure class (domestic hotel no-record / supplier cancellation); "Human in 60 Seconds" from day one. Audience: domestic hotels, top 50 Tier-2/3 cities, 10% A/B, Hindi + English — excludes flights, cabs, international, myBiz. *Gate:* ≥85% re-confirmation success on API supply, ≥95% adjudication accuracy vs human audit, no rise in fraudulent claims; **if coverage <60% of room nights, stop and re-scope to the agentic-voice path**. **Phase 2 (M3–9) — Close coverage, go proactive**: agentic outbound voice/WhatsApp to front desks in local languages; Disruption Prediction live + Supplier Reliability Score; expand to domestic flights, intercity cabs, redBus/Goibibo (ideal second surface — high frequency, low ticket value, fast learning); raise refund ceiling; all 8 languages; fast-follow Bharosa Mode fed by Guardian telemetry; open supplier SLA/chargeback negotiations. *Gate:* ≥50% AI resolution, test-cell repeat rate above control, SLA acceptance from top 200 partners. **Phase 3 (M9–18) — Scale, monetize, export**: 100% rollout across MMT/Goibibo/redBus; international + myBiz (24×7 multi-timezone, multi-currency refunds); launch "MMT Assure" (baseline free, paid premium tiers for int'l, bundled into MMT Black); export the guarantee as a signal into the OpenAI/agentic-commerce integration; reallocate ~10% of marketing to "Verified before you arrive. Refunded to your card, not your wallet."; publish re-confirmation, AI-resolution and refund-to-source metrics quarterly.
- **Visuals**: Three-column Gantt-style phase timeline (months 0–18) with diamond milestone markers at each gate, and a widening audience band beneath showing 10% traffic → multi-category → 100% across all three brands.

## Slide 13: Risks & Mitigations
- **Headline**: The Biggest Risk Is Shipping More AI Into the Exact Moment Users Already Hate
- **Purpose**: Demonstrate pre-mortem thinking on the risks that would actually kill this, including the self-inflicted one.
- **Convey**: Each major risk has a concrete, sequenced mitigation already built into the plan — the badge is never shown on unverifiable inventory, and the human escape hatch ships first.
- **Content**: Five-row risk table — Risk / Severity / Mitigation. (1) **Supplier coverage gap** (highest — it's the entire thesis): if only chains can be verified, Guardian works where the problem is smallest. → Phase 1 tests API supply only with a ≥60% room-night gate; Phase 2 agentic voice closes the long tail; where neither works, Supplier Reliability Score demotes or de-lists chronic failures; show honest "Verifying" state, never a false "Assured ✓". (2) **Auto-refund abuse + refund-float economics**: cash-by-default is a deliberate P&L hit and an auto-approver invites gaming. → value-capped auto-approval, claims-fraud model on booking/device/repeat-claim history, human review above cap, cost recovered via supplier SLA chargebacks; frame internally as substituting refund cost for CAC. (3) **Over-automation backfires**: more AI on the surface users hate confirms the grievance. → "Human in 60 Seconds" ships Phase 1; one-tap "talk to a person" on every Guardian message, never buried; publish median emergency escalation time (<60s) as a launch commitment. (4) **Regulatory**: RBI refund timelines, CCPA India dark-pattern/deficient-service guidance, DPDP consent for monitoring and outbound supplier calls. → full audit trail per automated decision, legal-signed published terms defining "Assured," explicit opt-in, data minimization; note cash-by-default *reduces* dark-pattern exposure vs forced wallet credit. (5) **Ixigo responds fast / claims first-mover**: TARA is live. → don't compete on assistant quality; compete on supplier verification and SLA enforcement, which require supply leverage a 7–9% player lacks; move fast on the Phase 2 voice handshake and publish coverage metrics quarterly.
- **Visuals**: Risk table with severity chips (High/Med) color-coded; small 2x2 likelihood-vs-impact scatter in the corner placing the five risks, with "supplier coverage gap" flagged as the one gating the whole thesis.
